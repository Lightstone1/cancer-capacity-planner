How the model works

Demand. Weekly urgent suspected cancer referrals start at each site's baseline, grow at the annual rate you set and, when seasonality is on, follow a typical monthly pattern (higher in January and March, lower in August and December). The scenario demand change applies on top.

Capacity. First appointment or straight-to-test slots per week, adjusted by the scenario capacity change and any extra slots from a time-limited initiative. Seasonality also reduces capacity in bank-holiday months. Slots lost to DNAs and late cancellations are taken off; those patients are rebooked and stay on the waiting list.

Queue. The model tracks every weekly cohort of referrals and books them first in, first out. The current waiting list is spread evenly across past weeks so its average age matches the current average wait you enter.

Performance. For each cohort seen, the time to first appointment is known. The time from first appointment to diagnosis outcome, and from diagnosis to first treatment, varies around the medians you enter (a log-normal spread set by pathway variability). The predicted FDS is the share of patients whose referral-to-outcome time is 28 days or fewer. The 62-day figure applies the same logic to the patients who convert to cancer.

Slots needed. The planner searches for the smallest number of core weekly slots that brings the FDS in the last 4 forecast weeks up to target. If even same-week appointments cannot reach the target, the limit is the diagnostic stage, and the planner shows the median diagnostic time you would need instead.

Limits. This is a planning model. It does not model individual diagnostic tests, inter-provider transfers, clock pauses, or patients who choose to wait. Calibrate it against your own recent FDS and 62-day results before relying on it.
