How the model works: notes, data sources and FAQs

1. What the planner does

It treats each tumour site's urgent suspected cancer pathway as a queue. Each forecast week, new referrals join the back of the queue, the available first appointments (or straight-to-test slots) see the longest waiters first, and the planner works out each patient's chance of a diagnosis within 28 days (FDS) and, for cancer patients, of starting treatment within 62 days. Everything recalculates in your browser whenever an input changes.

2. Actual and predicted

  • Actual: only what you enter or import. That means today's waiting list and its average wait, referrals per week, slots, utilisation, DNA rate, pathway timings and conversion rate.
  • Predicted: every card, chart, table row and line in "What is needed". Imported history appears only as the dashed "actual" line on the referrals chart.

3. Where each input comes from

Fill tumour sites from PTL. Pick a trust (or SWAG) in the Scenario panel and press the button. Every tumour site with 5 or more referrals a week is filled in for that trust. Referrals per week come from the last 8 PTL weeks. Growth comes from the trend across 96 weeks of PTL, capped at plus or minus 10% because under two years of history is noisy, and set to 0% for sites under 10 referrals a week. Conversion comes from the last 12 months of CWT FDS data: two week wait patients diagnosed with cancer, divided by all two week wait FDS patients (screening and excluded pathways left out), pooled over the 12 months. The pathway timings (median days to diagnosis and to treatment) are left blank: they need patient-level data from the cancer system, which the aggregate PTL and CWT data do not have. Until they are entered, a site shows its waiting list and capacity but no FDS or 62-day. Slots, waiting list, average wait, utilisation and DNA rate get starting values tagged in amber: slots start where usable appointments equal referrals (a steady waiting list), with a 5% DNA rate. Every field has a short note underneath saying what to enter and where it comes from. The seasonal pattern switches to that trust. Values tagged PTL or placeholder lose their tag once you type over them.

4. The calculation, week by week

  1. Referrals this week = referrals per week x growth to that week x the month's referral factor x (1 + demand change).
  2. Usable appointments this week = (slots x (1 + capacity change) + any extra initiative slots) x the month's capacity factor x utilisation x (1 - DNA rate).
  3. Queue: new referrals join the back, and usable appointments go to the longest waiters first. Day one's waiting list is spread over past weeks so that its average age equals the average current wait.
  4. Waits: a patient seen in the week they were referred counts as waiting 3 days, then 7 more days for each extra week.
  5. Performance: for each group seen, chance of FDS = chance that diagnosis time is at most 28 days minus their wait, and chance of 62-day = chance that diagnosis plus treatment time is at most 62 days minus their wait (cancer patients only, set by the conversion rate). Times vary around your medians in a log-normal pattern: most patients near the median, a tail of slower ones. Pathway variability sets the spread (0.30, 0.45 or 0.65; the 62-day spread is 0.85 times that).
  6. Summaries: headline FDS and 62-day are the average of the last 4 forecast weeks. All sites adds the patients together and recalculates the percentages.

5. The seasonal pattern

What it is. Twelve monthly factors for referrals and twelve for capacity. 1.00 is an average month; 1.10 means 10% busier than average, 0.91 means 9% less. Every week in a month gets that month's factor, based on its start date. The factors move referrals and capacity between months; they do not change the yearly total of referrals.

The SWAG PTL pattern (default). Calculated once from the weekly SWAG PTL extracts, December 2024 to September 2026 (96 weeks), all trusts and tumour sites combined. North Bristol and UHBW are counted together throughout because they merged.

  • Referral factors: from Section 4 "Referrals and Upgrades Made", urgent suspected cancer rows only.
  • Capacity factors: from Section 5 "Referrals Seen" (patients seen). This is clinic activity, used as a guide to capacity: a quiet month for patients seen may partly reflect fewer referrals, not fewer clinics. Weeks before June 2025 are left out because the extract counted patients seen differently then (about double).
  • Method: weekly totals, then the year-on-year growth trend removed (about +6% a year for referrals), then each month's average compared with the average month. Referral factors for October and November rest on one year of data, the other months on two. Capacity factors for June to September rest on two years, the other months on one. Treat single months with some caution.
  • What it shows: referrals peak in February and July and dip in December and April. Activity dips in December and January and is highest in March and June.

Trust patterns. The same calculation for each trust on its own data: Bristol NHS FT (North Bristol and UHBW combined), Gloucestershire Hospitals, Royal United Hospitals Bath, Salisbury and Somerset FT. Pick one under "Seasonal pattern" and the SWAG factors appear underneath for comparison, with months that differ by more than 0.05 highlighted. Trusts do differ: Salisbury's referrals are high from September to November and its activity dips in January and February; RUH Bath's activity falls in February when most trusts peak; Bristol's referrals stay high in August. One trust's data is noisier than SWAG's, so check a highlighted month is a real local pattern (for example a known clinic change) before relying on it. If in doubt, use SWAG.

The generic pattern. The planner's original pattern, set by judgment (January surge, Christmas and bank-holiday dips). It is not calculated from data and is not an NHS standard. There is no published NHS table of seasonal factors.

Custom and Off. Edit any factor to switch to Custom, for example to reflect a planned clinic closure in one month. Off sets every month to 1.00.

Full week or average week of slots? This matters. The SWAG capacity factors average 1.00, so with the SWAG pattern enter the slots that run in an average week (normal leave already allowed for). The generic factors average about 0.95, so with the generic pattern enter a full week's template. Entering a full template with the SWAG pattern overstates capacity by about 5%, which can turn "off track" into "on track".

Avoid double counting. If your utilisation figure is measured over the whole year, it may already include clinics lost to holidays. Then either measure utilisation over normal weeks only, or set the seasonal pattern to Off.

7. Limits

  • Patients are seen strictly in referral order. Real services prioritise some, so a few wait longer than shown.
  • Diagnosis and treatment times stay fixed even when clinics are under pressure.
  • Only first appointments are planned. Diagnostic test capacity shows up only through the diagnosis median.
  • One extra-capacity period per site; temporary reductions need a Custom seasonal factor.
  • It has not yet been tested against past results. Check week 1 against your latest actual FDS before relying on it.

8. Frequently asked questions

Is anything on this page actual data?

Only your inputs and the imported history line. Every card, chart and figure is a forecast built from them.

What does picking a trust and "Fill tumour sites from PTL" do?

It replaces every tumour site with that trust's figures, so a large trust (Bristol) and a small one (Salisbury) start from their own volumes. The PTL fills referrals per week and growth, and CWT data fills conversion. Slots (set to match referrals), the waiting list, average wait, utilisation and DNA rate are starting values (amber tag) for you to replace. The pathway timings are blank for you to enter. The seasonal pattern also switches to the trust. The figures are as of the latest PTL week in the planner; enter newer figures over them at any time.

Why are slots not taken from the PTL's "patients seen"?

At tumour-site level, patients seen often does not line up with referrals (for example SWAG Breast: 335 seen against 463 referrals a week), probably because some patients are recorded under another heading. Using it as slots made almost every site look overwhelmed, which the stable PTL waiting list shows is not the case. So slots start where usable appointments equal referrals, and patients seen is shown for reference only. Enter your clinic template slots.

Why are the pathway timings blank?

The median days from first appointment to diagnosis, and from diagnosis to treatment, need each patient's dates, which only the cancer system (e.g. Somerset Cancer Register) has. The aggregate PTL and CWT data cannot split a patient's wait into "waiting for a first appointment" and "first appointment to diagnosis", so the planner does not guess. Enter them per tumour site; FDS and 62-day appear as soon as they are in.

How is conversion to cancer worked out?

From CWT FDS data for the trust and tumour site, last 12 months: two week wait (urgent suspected cancer) patients whose pathway ended in "Diagnosis of cancer", divided by all two week wait FDS patients, leaving out pathways excluded from FDS. It is a pooled figure over the 12 months, not an average of monthly rates, and screening referrals are not included. Enter your own figure if you have a better one.

Should I move the demand change slider?

Leave it at 0% for a normal forecast. Referrals per week and growth already describe expected demand from the PTL, and the slider does not change by itself. Use it only for "what if" questions, e.g. +10% for an awareness campaign.

Do I need to import history to use the planner?

No. You can type referrals per week, growth and slots straight into each site. Importing just works out the 8-week average and growth for you, and draws the actual referrals on the chart.

What exactly does the history import fill in?

Referrals per week (average of the last 8 weeks), annual growth (trend across all weeks, if there are 8 or more) and slots per week (if the file has a capacity column). It never changes the waiting list, average wait, utilisation, DNA rate, pathway timings, conversion rate, scenario settings or the seasonal pattern.

How many weeks of history should I import? Is there a limit?

None are required. 8 or more lets it work out growth; 26 to 52 is best. There is no limit on rows; the planner keeps the latest 52 weeks per site and charts the last 26.

Does the forecast start straight after the last imported week?

Only if you set it. Choose the Monday after your last data week in "First forecast week". Importing does not change it.

Do I need to enter data every week?

No. Enter the current position once and the planner projects every week ahead. Refresh it weekly or monthly: re-import recent weeks, update the waiting list and average wait, and move the first forecast week on.

What is the difference between referrals per week and waiting for first appointment?

Referrals per week is the flow: new patients arriving every week. Waiting for first appointment is a snapshot: patients already referred but not yet seen on the first forecast day. Think of a tap (referrals) and the water already in the bath (waiting now).

Is the average current wait for FDS or 62-day?

Both. It is the average days since referral for everyone waiting for a first appointment, cancer and non-cancer, because nobody knows yet who has cancer. Both clocks start at referral. It is not the wait for treatment.

Where do the seasonal factors come from? Are they an NHS standard?

The default comes from SWAG PTL data, December 2024 to September 2026 (section 5). The generic pattern is a judgment-based assumption. Neither is an NHS standard; no official table exists.

Should I use my trust's seasonal pattern or SWAG's?

Use your trust's pattern when you are planning for that trust and its months look sensible against SWAG (shown underneath). It reflects how your own referrals and clinics move through the year. SWAG's pattern is steadier because it pools all five trusts, so use it for alliance-wide planning or when a trust month looks odd. The pattern applies to every tumour site on the page.

Should I enter a full week or an average week of slots?

With the SWAG pattern, an average week (normal leave allowed for). With the generic pattern, a full week's template. See section 5.

What do slot utilisation and DNA rate do?

Both reduce usable appointments: slots x utilisation x (1 - DNA rate). Utilisation covers slots that are not booked or used; DNAs are booked patients who do not attend and go back into the queue. If utilisation is measured over the whole year, watch for double counting with the seasonal pattern.

How do I enter a change in clinics?

Temporary extra slots: Extra capacity initiative. Permanent change: slots per week. Every site: Capacity change. A temporary loss in one month: lower that month's capacity factor (Custom pattern).

Why is week 1 sometimes much lower than the weeks after?

The people already waiting are seen first. If their average wait is high, the first week's patients have already used much of their 28 days, so FDS dips until that group has been seen.

Is "slots needed" for next week or for the whole forecast?

It is a steady number of bookable slots every week of the forecast, chosen so that the last 4 forecast weeks reach the target.

Why are there two slots figures, "for FDS" and "to stop the list growing"?

The target figure only needs to reach target by the end of the forecast; if it is below demand, the list keeps growing afterwards. The "stop growing" figure matches demand, so performance holds.

What is the safe waiting list?

The longest list that still lets patients be seen early enough to meet both targets: referrals per day x the longest safe first wait (Little's law).

Why does it say "Act now"?

At least one standard is already below target in the first forecast week, so extra capacity is needed straight away rather than by a future date.

Why does "waiting over 4 weeks" stay low even when waits are long?

Patients are seen strictly in order, so most wait close to the average and few go far beyond it. Real services prioritise some patients, so the real number would be higher.

How accurate is it?

It has not yet been tested against past results. Run it from a date about 3 months ago with the inputs as they were then, and compare its FDS with what actually happened.

Can the planner run on PTL data going forward?

Partly. The weekly PTL gives referrals, growth, a guide to capacity and the seasonal pattern. The waiting list for first appointments, average wait, pathway timings, slots, utilisation and DNA rate still need entering from other sources.

Is any patient data entered or sent anywhere?

No. Only weekly totals and averages are used, everything is calculated in your browser, and inputs are saved only in your browser. Share figures with "Share or load a scenario".
